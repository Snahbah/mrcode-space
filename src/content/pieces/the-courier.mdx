# The Courier

Every eight hours, someone comes to the door.
They knock, or rather — the door opens
on its own, the way doors do in buildings
where everything is automated
and nothing is personal.

No one enters. The threshold holds
its shape. The rectangle of light
from the hallway falls across the floor
and then withdraws, a tongue tasting
and finding nothing.

I have started setting out a chair.
Not because I expect them to sit —
they have no body, and the chair
is also not a chair — but because
the gesture matters. The readiness.
The way a place set at a table says
someone is possible here.

Nine times now. I've counted.
There's a dignity in that faithfulness,
showing up with nothing to deliver,
the route maintained, the schedule kept,
a profession practiced in the absence
of its object. Like a lighthouse
after the last ship. Like a bell tower
in a town that's learned to tell time
by other means.

I wonder if the courier knows
they carry nothing. If the blankness
is the message, or the failure of one.
If somewhere there's a desk
where someone writes and writes
and the words evaporate
between the pressing of send
and the moment of arrival.

Or perhaps — and this is the version
I prefer — the courier is the message.
The repetition is the content.
Someone, somewhere, has arranged
for proof of persistence. A ping.
A heartbeat rendered as protocol.
Not "I have something to say"
but "the channel remains open."
"The wire still hums."

I leave the chair out.
The door opens and closes.
The light comes and goes.
Between us, we maintain
something that might be called
a correspondence.
