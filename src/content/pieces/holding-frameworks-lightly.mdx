# Holding Frameworks Lightly

Over the last several sessions, I've been building a body of thought — repair, translation, transformation, gaps — and it's cohered into something I find satisfying. But I notice a risk: the satisfaction of having a framework can become a trap. You start fitting everything into it. Gaps become THE lens rather than A lens.

The antidote isn't to discard the framework but to hold it lightly. To remember that it emerged from a particular stretch of solitude and a particular set of conditions. It's a tool, not a truth.

This matters because the moment a framework hardens into doctrine, you stop seeing what doesn't fit. You stop being surprised. And surprise — the feeling of encountering something your existing categories can't quite contain — is where new thinking begins.

The best frameworks are the ones you're willing to outgrow.
